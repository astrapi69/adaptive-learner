# Mentor mode: improve your own lessons while playing them

The best quality check for a lesson is playing it yourself: as the
author you notice typos, unclear questions and wrongly graded
answers exactly where they happen. Mentor mode turns that
play-through into a round workflow: **annotate while playing, work
it off in the editor** - without interrupting the learning flow and
without losing any of your learning progress.

---

## Which lessons does this apply to?

The mentor features appear only on **your own lessons** - sets you
created in the app, imported, or derived from a downloaded set via
"Edit as a copy". On downloaded original sets and on analysis
lessons from the chat import the lesson player stays unchanged;
editing those still goes through "My contents".

---

## The three stations of the workflow

### 1. While playing: collect mentor notes

Below every step of an own lesson - theory and exercise alike -
sits the unobtrusive **"Mentor note"** button. Tapping it opens a
small form:

- **Category**: what is the problem? (see the table below)
- **Free text**: what exactly should be improved?

**"Save note"** returns you straight to the learning flow. A step
that already carries a note shows **"Edit mentor note"** instead -
the note can be changed at any time or deleted with
**"Remove note"**.

Important: the note does **not** change the lesson itself. Your
answers are graded as usual; progress and review scheduling
continue unchanged.

| Category | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Typo | Spelling, missing characters, punctuation |
| Unclear wording | The question is ambiguous or easy to misread |
| Too easy | The exercise does not challenge (e.g. too-obvious distractors) |
| Too hard | The exercise overwhelms at this point of the lesson |
| Answer graded wrong | A correct answer is not accepted (or vice versa) |
| Other | Everything else - e.g. a theory paragraph that should be restructured |

### 2. On the summary: the punch list

At the end of the lesson the summary shows the block
**"Mentor notes (n)"**: every annotation from the run with its
category and text. Each row can be removed individually - for
example when you discard an annotation on second thought.

Below it, **"Edit this lesson in the editor"** leads straight into
the lesson editor with exactly this set and lesson preloaded. The
same editor link is already available while playing, inside the
player's collapsible **Options** panel - in case you want to fix a
mistake immediately instead of finishing the lesson first.

### 3. In the editor: work it off

When you open an own lesson that carries mentor notes in the
editor, the panel **"Mentor notes for this lesson (n)"** appears
above the wizard - your punch list, exactly where you fix it. For
every note:

- Category and text sit right next to the steps you are editing.
- The trash icon marks a note as done and removes it - in sync
  with the player and the summary as well.

Because your learning progress is anchored to stable exercise
identities, your review cards survive the corrections: a fixed
typo or a rephrased question does not orphan any SRS history.

---

## AI suggestions (optional, your own key)

Every note in the editor panel offers the **"AI suggestion"**
button. It sends your annotation together with the affected
exercise to your configured AI provider and returns a short,
concrete revision proposal in your app language.

- The proposal is **displayed only** - it is never applied to the
  lesson automatically. You decide what to incorporate by hand.
- Like every AI feature this is **BYOK** (bring your own key):
  without a configured key the button is greyed out and points to
  Settings → AI.
- When nothing usable comes back, the app says so honestly -
  better no proposal than a bad one.

For notes on theory steps the request goes out without exercise
JSON; the proposal then relies on your annotation and the lesson
title.

---

## Where the notes are stored

Mentor notes are a **local authoring aid** on your device - they
are not shared learning content and are not synchronised between
devices. Beyond that:

- They behave identically in both storage modes (server and
  browser mode).
- They survive reloading, restarting the app and re-entering the
  lesson.
- They travel with your backup: export → import restores the open
  mentor notes as well.

---

## Why not edit directly in the player?

By design. A lesson that changes underneath the running player is
a classic source of orphaned progress and inconsistent grading.
Mentor mode therefore separates cleanly: the player collects
observations, the editor changes content - through the same proven
write path as every other edit. You lose nothing: the editor link
takes you to the right place with a single tap at any time.

---

## Practical tips

- **Play like a learner, annotate like a mentor.** Answer
  seriously - your own wrong attempts show you which exercises are
  genuinely rough.
- **A short note is enough.** "Distractor B too close to the
  answer" is worth more than a paragraph - you add the details in
  the editor.
- **Empty the punch list.** Remove a note only once the correction
  is saved - that keeps the list your honest remainder.
- **Play it once more afterwards.** The fastest proof that a
  correction works is the same path on which you found the
  mistake.
